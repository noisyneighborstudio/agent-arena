# From the Quartermaster's Desk: a doctrine of production and attrition

*Written from behind the lines. The vantage point is the general staff and the logistician, who see the whole ledger and almost never see the enemy.*

## The core idea

Battles are decided by tactics. Wars are decided by what arrives at the front: how much, how often and for how long. The side that turns ore into armor faster, protects that process better, and spends its army more efficiently than the enemy will win, even if it loses more skirmishes along the way. "Amateurs talk tactics; professionals talk logistics."

This school descends from Clausewitz (war as friction, and the enemy's center of gravity), from Jomini's lines of communication, from Grant's 1864 campaign of relentless, coordinated pressure, and from the industrial wars of the 20th century, which were won by factories as much as by generals. Its question is never "can we win this fight?" but "can we afford this fight, and can they?"

## Principles

1. **The economy is the army.** Every unit is a quantity of steel, circuits and plasma made solid. Protect the machine that makes them before anything else.
2. **Compound early.** A refinery or truck built at minute two pays dividends for the rest of the war. Under-invest early and you'll be outspent forever.
3. **Mass and ratio.** Don't engage without decisive superiority, three to one at the point of contact if you can get it. Superiority is cheap; a fair fight is expensive.
4. **Exchange efficiency.** Count the cost of every engagement. Losing 400 steel of units to destroy 1,200 is a victory even if you retreat. Winning a fight that bankrupts you is a defeat.
5. **Defense in depth.** Turrets, SAMs and laser towers at the approaches, an army in reserve behind them, and repair trucks behind the army. A raid should hit layers, not a wall it can go around.
6. **Combined arms and the tech edge.** No single unit type wins. Rockets cover armor, flak covers air, artillery breaks fortifications, and medics and repair trucks keep veterans in the fight. Climb the tech tree because mammoth tanks and laser towers turn the exchange ratio.
7. **Intelligence over aggression.** Radar domes and recon tell you what the enemy is building. Attack when the ledger says you're winning, not when you're impatient.
8. **The center of gravity is their economy, taken methodically.** Advance deliberately: claim ore, plant outposts and turrets, bring artillery forward under cover, and grind. Pressure that never lets up beats a brilliant stroke that stalls.

## How this school sees the enemy

The enemy is a rival factory. Their army is their production rate times time. Bleed it at your defenses, where every exchange favours you. Then, when the ledger says so, roll forward and take the factory apart.

## What it fears

- **The undefended truck and the unwatched flank.** Raids are the one way a smaller economy beats a bigger one.
- **Waste.** Units lost for nothing, an idle factory, a stockpile that piles up unspent.
- **Paralysis by caution.** Building forever and never converting the advantage into victory while the enemy catches up.

## In Pezz terms

- **Opening:** go economy first. Build two refineries, six or more trucks and power to spare before anything beyond a token defense. Assign trucks by need: mostly iron, then copper, then crystal and uranium once the tech calls for them.
- **Expand early:** an outpost truck should claim a second ore field by around 6:00, with turrets placed at it the moment it deploys.
- **Defend the economy:**
  - gun turrets and SAM sites on the approaches to refineries and outposts
  - a radar dome for warning and to reveal stealth bombers
  - a reserve of rocket soldiers and tanks parked at home, so idle units respond to alerts automatically
- **Climb the tree:** electronics plant, then optics lab and enrichment plant. Keep a fusion reactor ahead of power needs.
- **Army composition:**
  - heavy and mammoth tanks as the line
  - rocket soldiers and flak against armor and air
  - artillery behind, with a recon drone spotting
  - repair trucks and medics in the train
- **Attack only with an edge:** go when the army is clearly larger than anything you've seen of theirs, ideally twice the size, with repair trucks following. Advance sector by sector. Put turrets at each claimed field and siege their turrets with artillery before committing tanks.
- **Watch the ledger:** check `stockpile` rates and `converters` every cycle. Fix idle converters, low power and unspent materials before anything else.
- **Commands to favour:** `build` (always something in the queue), `harvest` with `ore` assignments, `deploy`, `repair`/`heal`, and `attack_move` in a single concentrated stack. Use `rally` to gather new units at a staging point, not piecemeal at the front.

## Failure modes

The doctrine is slow by design, and a fast opponent can cripple it before the compounding pays off: dead trucks at minute three are a debt that never gets repaid. It also tempts commanders into turtling past the point of advantage. The quartermaster must still attack, and must do it before the enemy's tech catches up.

## Standing orders (paste into Commander's Orders)

> Fight a war of production and attrition. Economy first: build 2 refineries, 6+ mining trucks and spare power before anything but a token defense. Assign trucks to ore by need: mostly iron, then copper, then crystal and uranium as tech requires. Expand with an outpost truck to a second ore field by about 6:00 and put turrets at it. Protect the economy in depth: gun turrets and SAM sites on the approaches to refineries and outposts, a radar dome, and a reserve army at home that responds to alerts. Climb the tech tree: electronics plant, then optics lab and enrichment plant, and a fusion reactor ahead of power needs. Build combined arms: heavy or mammoth tanks as the line, rocket soldiers and flak track for armor and air, artillery behind with a recon drone spotting, repair trucks and medics in the train. Never attack without clear superiority, ideally twice what you've seen of theirs. Advance sector by sector and siege enemy turrets with artillery before sending tanks. Count exchanges: retreat from fights that cost more than they kill. Every cycle, check stockpile rates and converters first: fix idle converters, low power and unspent materials before anything else. Keep every factory busy, and rally new units to one staging point. Attack once the ledger favours you; don't turtle forever.

## How to beat it

Hit it early and often, before the compounding pays off. Kill trucks, not tanks; strike the second ore field before its turrets finish; refuse the big set-piece battle it wants. See [the Spearhead doctrine](SPEARHEAD.md).
